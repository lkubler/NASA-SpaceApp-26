## 3. What is real, what is model, what is simulated, what is assumed
- **Real (NASA / partner data, unchanged in substance):** the Curiosity images behind the Access Mars terrain (the mesh and its height/slope grid are labelled model in the app); Access Mars points and texts; Curiosity photos (PIA21042, PIA20174, PIA18476); Curiosity waypoints (crater view drive and sites); HiRISE slope grid (1 m); HRSC Gale relief (ESA, fitted to MOLA); Mars globe; star map; RAD dose rate; REMS temperatures; document limits (slope 20 deg, 2 / 3.5 km, 1.2x, 6 / 8 h, 1 h secondary O2, 2 h to shelter, antenna heights of suit and rover, pace, masses, backpack 350 W / 8 h, signal delay range, gravity, sol length).
- **Model (our calculation):** slope-closed mask, radio-shadow mask (geometric line of sight), candidate sample spots, Dijkstra routes and cost fields, work rate (Pandolf scaled to Mars, anchored at Apollo 1200 BTU/h), consumable capacities and use, reserve on return, turn-back point, turn-back line on the small map, live route in the walk, time to shelter, the HiRISE slope caution (alignment about 2 m), dose during the walk, ground colour fill, sky colour, "EVA zone" frame.
- **Simulated (no data):** heart rate, core temperature, suit pressure, helmet CO2; the radio relay device (its effect on contact is calculated as line of sight); Plan rover drive line.
- **Assumption:** demo time factor 76x, spot work 20 min, screen speed 5x, fast walk 1.5x, eye height 1.7 m, relay antenna 3 m, contact weight 0.5, the rover as storm shelter, 09:00 start, signal delay 18 min, terrain coefficient 1.2, standing work rate, cooling-water capacity, rover at square centre and its 4.5 m length, base at Bradbury Landing, crater height 2.5x, max 3 stops.

## 4. Data-driven chain (what the code does)
1. `core/terrain.js` loads the Access Mars walk grid: 256 x 256 cells of 0.5 m, height (m) and slope (deg), precomputed offline from the mesh.
2. `planner.js isClosed`: every cell steeper than the 20 deg rule (EVA-EXP-0042) is closed. `radio.js radioShadowMask`: for every cell, a straight line from the rover antenna (2 m) to the suit antenna (1.83 m) is tested against the terrain; a cell has contact if it sees the rover, or sees the relay (3 m mast, placed by the visitor on the Plan map) while the relay sees the rover; otherwise radio shadow.
3. `planner.js findRoute`: Dijkstra over 8 neighbours, edge cost = 3D distance between cell centres, times 1.5 where the step ends in radio shadow (contact weight, assumption; the distance and time shown stay the real ones), closed cells only at a 1e5 penalty (then the route is marked as breaking the slope rule). One leg per rover -> stop -> ... -> rover.
4. `planner.js stepCost` + `suit.js workRate`: each step's slope sets the work rate (Pandolf with 82 kg body, 23 kg load, 2 km/h, scaled by 3.71 / 9.81, anchored at 1200 BTU/h; downhill counts as flat); time = distance / pace x 76.
5. `plan.js planNumbers`: walks the route, draws oxygen, CO2 and water by energy and the battery by time, adds 20 min work per stop, and computes distance, climb, steepest slope, farthest distance, radio-shadow stretches, dose, excursion time and the reserve on return (way home at 1.2x work rate); the first point where turning back leaves zero reserve is the turn-back point. The same numbers feed Plan, Go / no-go and Debrief.
6. Go / no-go (`3-go`): seven checklist rows with planned value, limit and status. Rules: slope, distance, way home, excursion length, and solar storm time to shelter (`datalines.js timeToShelter`: the longest time back to the rover from any point of the route, against 2 h). Cautions: radio shadow (time without contact, with the relay counted) and slope in HiRISE (`datalines.js hiriseSlopeOnRoute`: metres of the route over 20 deg in the 1 m model). In demo time the far spot breaks the storm rule (2 h 37 against 2 h), so the rule really binds.
7. Walk (`4-walk/sim.js`): each frame, `hasRadio` at the current position, `isClosedAt` under foot and ahead, and `plan.js reserveFrom` using a cost field to the rover (`costField`) give radio status, steep warnings, reserve on return and the turn-back warning; off the planned leg the route is planned again from where the crew stands to the current target (same rules); the small map draws the turn-back line, the edge of the ground from which the crew still gets home with reserve (the same calculation per place, refreshed about once a second); the consumables are drawn with the same `workRate`.
8. Helmet extras: dose = 0.67 mSv/day x suit time; outside temperature interpolated from REMS sol 1440 by local time; body readings simulated from the work rate.

## 5. Gaps and weak spots (state after the last features)
- **Two datasets carry decisions, one of them only as a cross-check.** Every route, rule, reserve and warning comes from the Access Mars mesh (a 2016 reconstruction from Curiosity images, about 20 % smoother than the HiRISE DTM per `scripts/preprocess/README.md`). HiRISE now checks the same route in Go / no-go and warns where it sees steeper ground, but it does not close ground or change the route. Say "slopes from Access Mars, cross-checked against HiRISE", never "routes from HiRISE slopes".
- **Distance rules can never bind.** The site is 128 m; the farthest spot is about 60 m from the rover, while the limits are 2 km / 3.5 km. The rules that do bind on this site: slope (closes ground), way home / reserve, and solar storm time to shelter in demo time.
- **Demo time.** Suit time runs 76x faster so that a 128 m site produces hours of excursion. The storm rule and the reserve bind because of this factor; it is labelled as an assumption in the app.
- **The radio relay is simulated hardware.** Contact is geometric line of sight only (no radio physics). No public document gives a limit for time without contact, so radio shadow is a caution and a weight in the route, not a rule. The relay position is lost when the page is reloaded.
- **Rock type is not in our data.** The sandstone request, the sample spots and the photo at the spot are not linked by data (`places.js`: "Rock type is not in our data"). The Walk says the photo shows the rock "not this exact spot".
- **Visual-only data:** Gale crater HRSC view, Mars globe, star map, Curiosity drive line, REMS temperature, Access Mars point texts: none changes a route or a go / no-go. RAD dose is shown but is not a rule.
- **Most rule values are lunar / Apollo**, not Mars (slope, distances, pace, 1.2x, 6 / 8 h, work rates, antenna heights). The app flags them (`moon: true`). The terrain coefficient is from Wikipedia.
- **Fixed since the first version of this file:** the unused prepared files (about 6 MB) were removed; the Sources page entries for Access Mars, Curiosity waypoints, Curiosity photos, HiRISE and MOLA now claim only what the app loads and shows; the Plan terrain label is "model" (Access Mars is a reconstruction).
- **Not confirmed here:** the final compliance report (`reviews/compliance-final.md`) checks the Sources page against the shipped files.
